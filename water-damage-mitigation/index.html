Put simply, mitigation is the work of holding damage to its current size. Repair is a different job. It is everything done before the repair to protect what is still sound. In Rochester Hills, our team handles mitigation by controlling the source, removing the water, containing the wet area and stabilizing the structure. When this stage goes right, there is less to rebuild afterward, occasionally nothing at all.

People often use mitigation and restoration to mean the same thing. They are two phases. Mitigation is the emergency phase: stop the loss, limit the spread, stabilize the home. Once the structure tests dry, restoration takes over and covers repairing or rebuilding finishes, drywall and floors that were damaged. Insurers treat them as separate line items too, so it helps to know which phase you are in.

A typical mitigation visit covers four things. First is source control, which keeps more water from coming in. Second is extraction, to clear out standing water. Three: contain the area with plastic sheeting and managed airflow, so clean rooms stay free of moisture and contaminants. Fourth is stabilization: unsalvageable materials come out, surfaces receive antimicrobial treatment, and dehumidifiers and air movers are placed. Furniture and personal items are protected too, either relocated or raised on blocks.

Maintenance Tips for Water Damage Mitigation

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • In cold months, a heated house protects pipes from freezing, even while you travel.
  • Braided steel supply lines should take the place of rubber ones on the washing machine about every five years.
  • Clean the gutters regularly, and aim downspouts so they discharge away from the foundation.
  • Ground next to the foundation should fall away from it, which keeps water from collecting there.
  • A main shutoff that will not turn is no help, so locate and test yours.
  • Once a month, look beneath sinks and near toilets for stains, drips or soft spots in the floor.

Water Damage Mitigation, Step by Step

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's how we handle water damage mitigation from start to finish:

1

Stop the Water Source

Depending on the cause, our crew closes the supply, caps a failed line, or covers the entry point with tarps and boards. If a plumber or roofer is needed for the permanent fix, we coordinate the timing.

2

Remove Standing Water

Pumps and extractors take out the standing water, and we inspect the carpet and pad. Getting bulk water out quickly does more to limit damage than any amount of drying equipment alone.

3

Contain Wet Areas

Wet rooms are closed off from dry ones with barriers of plastic sheeting and controlled airflow. As a result, contaminants, dust and humid air stay put instead of spreading around the house mid-job.

4

Stabilize the Structure

Materials past saving come out, an antimicrobial is applied to surfaces, and air movers plus dehumidifiers go in. We log moisture readings each visit until the structure is ready for repairs, usually within 3 to 5 days.

When to Call for Water Damage Mitigation in Rochester Hills

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Baseboards are swelling or pulling away from the wall after a leak.
  • Rain is getting inside through a damaged roof or an open window gap.
  • The space under your cabinets in the bathroom or kitchen took on water you cannot get to.
  • Following a water loss, indoor air stays damp and condensation forms on the glass.
  • An adjuster or agent said you must reasonably limit additional damage.
  • Underfoot the carpet is spongy because the padding below holds water.
  • Wet materials have sat for a day and a musty smell is starting.

Why Professional Water Damage Mitigation Matters

Fast decisions, one after another, are what mitigation consists of. Can this section of drywall dry in place, or does it need cutting out? Is the water Category 1, 2 or 3, and has it changed since the leak began? Should the toe kick under the cabinets be pulled?

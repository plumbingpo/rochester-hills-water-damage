A sump pump that quits, a sewer that backs up, a pipe that lets go, rising river water, storm surge or a hard rain can each flood a property, and the damage should be handled by professionals without delay. In nearly every case the water falls into Category 2, known as gray water, or Category 3, known as black water, which means contaminants, bacteria and other hazards that are a real health risk. Day or night, 24/7, Rochester Hills Restoration sends flood cleanup crews throughout Rochester Hills to remove floodwater, sanitize the affected rooms and safely restore your home or building.

We start with fast water extraction, using truck-mounted and submersible pumps that can move thousands of gallons per hour. Next our technicians, who hold IICRC certification, judge the level of contamination and choose a sanitizing plan that covers HEPA air filtration, antimicrobial application and disinfecting each surface that got wet. Materials beyond saving, like contaminated drywall, damaged insulation and saturated carpet pad, are removed and disposed of the right way.

Once the space is extracted and sanitized, our team brings in commercial drying gear, namely low-grain refrigerant (LGR) dehumidifiers plus high-velocity air movers, so the structural components dry all the way through. Daily readings from calibrated moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera tell us when everything is fully dry, which is what keeps mold from growing. Every stage of the job is recorded for your claim: photographs, detailed moisture maps, logs of the equipment used and the scope-of-work reports that insurance adjusters require.

Signs You Need Flood Cleanup in Rochester Hills

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Cracked foundation walls, low doorways or window wells letting water inside
  • A water line on walls or baseboards showing how high it rose
  • A layer of silt, dirt or debris on the floor after water recedes
  • Basement flooding after the sump pump quit in a downpour
  • An overflowing washing machine, dishwasher or water heater
  • Living areas flooded by contaminated water from a backed-up drain or sewer
  • Standing water anywhere on your property, whatever the source

Flood Cleanup Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Roof runoff should leave the downspout far enough out that it cannot soak the foundation
  • Coat basement walls with waterproofing and seal any cracks in the foundation
  • A backwater valve on your sewer line keeps a backup from pushing sewage indoors
  • Keep documents and keepsakes in watertight bins on shelves, not on a basement floor
  • Being outside a designated flood zone does not rule out flooding, so think about flood coverage
  • In areas of the property that tend to flood, raise electrical systems and appliances off the floor

Why Hire a Professional for Flood Cleanup?

Flood cleanup is far more than pumping out standing water. The water brings sewage, chemicals, bacteria and more with it, and all of that is a serious hazard to health. Protecting your family from E. coli, hepatitis and similar waterborne illness takes proper containment, correct use of protective equipment (PPE) and proven sanitizing methods, all things professional teams train for.

Improper cleanup also tends to miss moisture you cannot see, and within days that trapped water can cause a major mold problem. With thermal imaging, psychrometric calculations and moisture meters, trained technicians confirm that subfloors, structural framing, wall cavities and every other wet area dry to an acceptable standard. Drying by measurement is the one dependable method for avoiding secondary damage, which can add thousands to the repair bill.

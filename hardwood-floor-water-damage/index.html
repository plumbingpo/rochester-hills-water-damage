Hardwood Floor Water Damage: What Rochester Hills Homeowners Should Know

It does not take long for water to change a wood floor. Moisture enters each board through its underside and edges, the boards swell and press together, and cupping begins as the edges lift above the middle. Cupping alone is no reason to give up on the floor. When drying starts within the first few days, solid hardwood frequently lies flat again. What matters is removing moisture from the wood and from its subfloor steadily and under control.

We dry hardwood with floor drying mats. A vacuum unit attached to the sealed mats lifts water out by way of the seams and the wood itself, and dehumidifiers hold the room air at a dry level. Compared with carpet or drywall it is slower, commonly a week or more. Sanding has to wait until the wood is fully dry. Sand a cupped floor too soon and it will crown later.

What It Looks Like

  • Puffy baseboards and casings next to the affected boards
  • Boards lifting off the subfloor or tenting upward at the seams
  • Crowning, meaning the middle of each board bulges above its edges
  • A washboard feel underfoot from edges that stand taller than board centers
  • A hazy, whitened or flaking finish where the water sat
  • New squeaks or popping sounds when you walk across the wet area

How We Fix It

Our Hardwood Floor Water Damage Process

1

Measure the Moisture

Our team meters the wet boards, the subfloor under them and a dry area of floor to compare against. The comparison shows how far down moisture reached and how likely the floor is to recover.

2

Remove Surface Water

Rugs and furnishings come off the affected boards while pooled water is vacuumed up at once. Boards swell less when the seams stop taking in water sooner.

3

Install the Mat System

Floor mats are taped down and connected to a vacuum system that pulls moisture up through the boards. To avoid cracking, dehumidifiers keep conditions steady and the wood dries at an even rate.

4

Monitor and Advise

Every day or two we take new readings, continuing until the subfloor and boards match the dry reference spots. Our final advice is one of three outcomes, no further work, swapping a few boards, or refinishing the floor.

Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Growth Beneath the Floor

Between the boards and the subfloor there is only darkness and still air. You will smell it before you see it, because nothing shows on the surface.

Rusting Fasteners

Lingering moisture rusts the fasteners, which then hold the boards less firmly. Dark marks from that rust soak into the boards and can be too deep to sand out.

Cracks From Rushed Drying

When the top of the wood dries much quicker than the inside, stress builds in the board. The result can be cracks, splits and permanent gaps between boards.

Wet Subfloor

The subfloor can hold moisture long after the boards feel dry. Unless that layer is dried as well, expect renewed cupping or mold on the underside.

The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

First hours

Water Goes Under

Most of the water ends up beneath the boards, having run down through the joints. You can towel off the top and still have a saturated underside.

Hours 24 to 48

Edges Begin to Rise

Boards swell and the edges rise. Finish may turn cloudy. Mold can begin on the subfloor and the unfinished underside of the wood.

Days 3 through 7

Pressure Builds Up

Expanding boards push harder and harder on one another. Edges get crushed together, nails work loose and parts of the floor may start rising from the subfloor.

Past one week

Permanent Damage

Black stains, buckled boards and mold beneath the flooring are now probable. By now replacement, not drying, is the answer for most of the affected section.

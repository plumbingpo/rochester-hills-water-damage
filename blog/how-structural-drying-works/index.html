The Science of Structural Drying in Rochester Hills, MI

Home / Blog / Understanding Professional Structural Drying in Rochester Hills, MI

Few Rochester Hills, MI homeowners know much about structural drying, even though the rest of a restoration job depends on it. Once the visible water is gone, a lot is still held in the building itself, in framing lumber, drywall, insulation, subflooring, and concrete. Mold, lingering smells, and deteriorating materials are the result when the trapped water is not taken out correctly. This article explains the science behind professional structural drying and why it produces far better results than DIY attempts.

What Psychrometry Means for Drying

Psychrometry, a science concerned with gas-vapor mixtures and their thermodynamic and physical properties, is what structural drying is built on. Put simply, it is about the way air movement, relative humidity, and temperature work together to pull water from wet materials. Every job is different, so technicians with IICRC certification calculate the setup psychrometrically, from air mover placement and target humidity and temperature to the number and kind of dehumidifiers.

Drying Equipment Built for the Job

Consumer dehumidifiers and fans do not compare to the machines a restoration crew brings. LGR (Low-Grain Refrigerant) dehumidifiers can remove 15 to 30 or more pints of moisture per hour from the air, compared to 2 to 3 pints per hour for a residential unit. High-velocity air movers create precisely directed airflow at 2,500 CFM or more to speed evaporation from wet surfaces. For special cases such as drying in cold weather or very heavy moisture loads, crews turn to desiccant dehumidifiers. All of it costs tens of thousands, and using it well takes specific training.

How a Drying Job Unfolds

There is a defined method behind every structural drying project. First, a thorough moisture assessment maps all affected areas using moisture meters and thermal imaging. This map guides equipment placement and establishes baseline moisture readings. Dehumidifiers and air movers are positioned according to psychrometric calculations to create the best conditions for evaporation and moisture removal. Temperature may be raised to increase evaporation rates. A technician returns daily to take moisture readings at all monitoring points, adjust equipment as needed, and document progress. Drying continues until all affected materials reach acceptable moisture levels compared to dry reference standards.

How Many Days Will Drying Take?

Most residential structural drying projects take 3 to 5 days with proper commercial equipment. How long it takes comes down to how much moisture there is, which materials got wet (drywall dries far faster than concrete), how the building is constructed, the ambient conditions, and how many machines of what type are running. Typical figures are 5 to 7 days or more for concrete, 3 to 4 days for wood framing, and 2 to 3 days for standard drywall. Your technician will provide a projected timeline based on initial moisture readings and adjust it as drying progresses.

Why Monitoring Matters

The difference between a professional job and guessing is monitoring every day. Without calibrated moisture readings, there is no way to know whether materials are actually drying, drying at the expected rate, or have reached safe moisture levels. Deep in a material, moisture can remain at a dangerous level even though the surface feels dry when touched. Verifying it takes a moisture meter that has been calibrated. There is a paperwork benefit too: documented drying proves to an insurance company that the job met industry protocol, and that backs up your claim and your home's value.
